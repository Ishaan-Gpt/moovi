The look was the finding that changed my whole plan. I'd assumed the 3D channels were winning, because 3D looks expensive, but the two biggest character films in the genre are flat 2D drawings dressed up to feel 3D, with a rim of sunset light around every black head, a blurred foreground and a soft city behind, while the real 3D character channels sit lower, at around nine hundred thousand. I knew it was true because I'd already tried. Earlier the same day I gave Claude one hour to make a stickman short in proper 3D, it finished one in forty-seven minutes, and two blind judges scored it five out of ten, because the legs couldn't run and the hand couldn't reach the face. So this time we drew it in 2D on a single canvas, the way the top channels do, where nothing has to run or bend, and every shape sits exactly where you want it.
