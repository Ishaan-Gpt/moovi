So I asked Claude to watch the openings of the seven biggest ones the way a film student would, one frame every second, with the narration written out word by word, and to tell me what the hits do that the copies don't. It came back with a rulebook, and the first rule surprised me, because none of them tell you the premise straight away. Ifverse shows you a fly, and then a coffee cup that's caught before it has even started to fall, and only after thirty-five seconds of that does the title appear, because by then you've already seen the power working and you badly want to know how. The second rule is a number on screen, the normal person against you, a quarter of a second against two and a half hundredths, which turns a daydream into something you can measure. The third is a friend who says the line you're thinking, "how did you do that", so you never feel lost, and the fourth is the pace, because Ifverse reads at about one and a half words a second and lets the silences do half the work.
