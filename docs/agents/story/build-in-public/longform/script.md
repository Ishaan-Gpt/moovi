## 1 · the hook: proof and the stake

## 11 · the first try: 3D in one hour, 5/10
My first try was in proper 3D. I gave Claude one hour, and it finished a stickman short in forty-seven minutes, which sounds great until two blind judges scored it five out of ten. The legs couldn't run and the hand couldn't reach the face, so I went back to study the films that actually win.

## 2 · the gold rush
The genre is called What If, and it's barely a month old. Ifverse posted a seven-minute film about a kid in a red hoodie on the seventh of October, and it has now been watched four point six million times. Within three weeks the others arrived. Minute Zero got two point two million views, and Miko got almost seven hundred thousand with only seven thousand subscribers, the kind of number that makes people quit their jobs. By the third week a hundred and forty-seven of these went up, and the typical new video fell to twenty-one thousand views. Copy what you see, and you arrive just in time to share the leftovers. And yet fresh ideas still break out every week, and that gap is what I wanted Claude to find.

## 3 · the study and the rulebook
So I asked Claude to watch the openings of the seven biggest ones the way a film student would. It took one frame every second and wrote out every word of the narration. Then it came back with a rulebook, and the first rule surprised me. None of them tell you the premise straight away. Ifverse shows you a fly, then a coffee cup caught before it has even started to fall, and only after thirty-five seconds does the title appear, because by then you badly want to know how. The second rule is a number on screen, normal against you, a quarter of a second against two and a half hundredths. The third is a friend who says the line you're thinking, "how did you do that", and the fourth is the pace, about one and a half words a second, with the silences doing half the work.

## 4 · the look
The look was the finding that changed my plan. I'd assumed 3D was winning, because 3D looks expensive, but the two biggest character films here are flat 2D dressed up to feel 3D. Every black head has a rim of sunset light, the foreground is blurred, and a soft city sits behind, while the real 3D channels sit lower, at around nine hundred thousand. So this time we drew it in 2D on a single canvas, where nothing has to run or bend, and every shape sits exactly where you want it.

## 5 · the idea
The idea is where most copies die, so Claude searched every premise on my list before we wrote a word. "What if you could rewind ten seconds" has been made at least eight times this month, and the best of those has thirteen thousand views. "What if you only had a thousand words left for your whole life" hadn't been made by anyone in the genre, and the closest thing was a Roblox story with eighty-nine thousand views, so people want it and nobody had served it. It keeps the genre's best trick, a number over the head, and it asks the characters for nothing but talking, looking and going quiet, because every action you can't animate well is a scene you'll end up cutting.

## 6 · the build
The script came next, written the way these channels write, in the second person, with the power proven before it's named. You have forty-one words left, your best friend has just asked you to be his best man, and the whole café is staring at the number over your head. The voices came from Fish Audio's free model, and Claude lined up every word to a hundredth of a second, so the counter drops from forty-one to forty on exactly the word "always". The pictures are all code, a café three and a half thousand pixels wide with a sunset in the window, twenty-one shots, and music written in code, so there's nothing to license.

## 7 · the fall
Then I handed it to a judge who didn't know which film was mine. Claude gave a fresh agent three openings, mine, Ifverse's and Ifinite's, with no names on them. Mine came last, with four point three out of ten. The opening was the back of a black head with no face, the wedding ask was never shown, the light was a muddy olive, and the crowd stood around like furniture. The judge was right about every one of them.

## 8 · the climb and the win
So we fixed exactly what the judge named and nothing else. Your face and the number open the film now, the ring box sits between the two of you, the room got brighter, and the crowd reacts. Then a second judge, who had never seen the first version, scored mine five and a half, and the four-point-six-million-view opening four point eight, so in that round, mine won.
