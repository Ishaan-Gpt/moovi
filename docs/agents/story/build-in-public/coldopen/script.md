# "What If You Only Had 1,000 Words Left?": the cold open (step 3, draft 1)

Built against the rulebook (STUDY-whatif.md): the power is proven before it is named; one number, normal against you; one line of
disbelief from someone else; the title at about 0:30; a calm read. Our own scene, not Miko's opener.
Fact: people say about 16,000 words a day (Mehl et al., Science, 2007: 16,215 for women and 15,669 for men, with no real difference).

Voices: NARRATOR is Fish "Ethan". FRIEND is Fish "Alex" (one line). YOU is Fish "Verity" (one word). Three voices is a taste call, so
Ishaan is asked. The genre itself uses character lines ("How did you do that?").

| # | Who | Line | Picture (2D with depth, one light mood: a warm café at dusk) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NARRATOR | You have forty-one words left for the rest of your life, and your best friend has just asked you to be the best man at his wedding. | A café table at dusk. You, a black ball head in a moss-green jacket, and over your head a pill reading **41**. Across the table the friend, with a mustard scarf, holds out a ring box |
| 2 | FRIEND | Come on. Just one word. | A close-up of the friend, half laughing, half begging |
| 3 | NARRATOR | Everyone in the café has turned to look at the number over your head, because everyone can see it, and everyone knows exactly what a word from you costs. | A slow pan across the other tables, every head turned toward the pill |
| 4 | YOU | Always. | A close-up of you; on the word, the pill ticks **41 → 40** and dims one notch |
| 5 | NARRATOR | He cries into his cappuccino, which is fair, because that was one of the last forty words you will ever say out loud. | The friend's hand over his face; your hand slides a napkin across |
| 6 | NARRATOR | The average person says about sixteen thousand words a day and never thinks about a single one of them. You were given one thousand, for your whole life, on the morning you turned ten. | A split: a stranger's pill spinning past 16,000 against yours, frozen at 1,000, over a small boy at a birthday table |
| 7 | NARRATOR | So, what if you only had a thousand words left? | The title: WHAT IF YOU ONLY HAD 1,000 WORDS LEFT? over the café wide (about 0:30) |
| 8 | NARRATOR | The rules arrived that same morning, written in the fog on the bathroom mirror: every word you say out loud comes off the counter, a whisper costs the same as a shout, and writing things down costs nothing at all. | The fogged mirror, three lines written in it by an unseen finger; the boy reading them, toothbrush still in his mouth |
| 9 | NARRATOR | Which is why, by lunchtime, your mum had a drawer full of your notes, and you had already spent eleven words on a joke about the dog, which, to be fair, was a very good joke. | The kitchen drawer stuffed with notes; the pill at **989**; the dog looking pleased with itself |

Length: about 150 words; at a calm read (about 2.3 words a second) that's 60-65 s.
