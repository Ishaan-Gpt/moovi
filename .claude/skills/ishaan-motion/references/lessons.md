## Emotion, suspense, and losses that cost something (Ishaan, 2026-10-29, on the Stickman Games clip 3 rewrite)
"Don't rush in eliminating players... it is most important to make viewers emotionally engaged with the film and the characters,
and a lot of suspense, climax, drama, everything happening in there, and never simple easy losses... it keeps viewers hooked
straight up for the entire video." **Every game segment has an emotional spine, not just a rule: each featured character
wants something we can feel (a stake said in their own words before the game), every loss is a fight (they try, nearly make it,
and we see what it cost: an empty platform, someone looking down), the first loss is an event of its own rather than part of a
montage, a setup is paid off against expectation (the one who is "never wrong" is wrong), and the segment climbs to a climax
where a known character's fate hangs on a choice another character makes (a sacrifice beats a skill). Fewer exits, each heavier.
Comic release comes right after the tension, never instead of it. A script is judged blind for "would a 12-year-old feel
something and refuse to look away" before any voice is recorded.**

## Smooth means no pops (Ishaan, 2026-10-30, on the Gandhi Short: "Film is not smooth, many errors, fix them all")
**Before any film reaches Ishaan, scan the exported MP4 for pops and look at every shot in motion, not only at mid-shot stills.**
- **Pop scan:** frame-difference every frame (ffmpeg `tblend=difference` + `signalstats` YAVG at 270x480). Flag any frame more than about
  2.6x the median of its 12 neighbours that is not a cut or a transition. Each flag is a snap to fix at the source.
- **Motion strips:** 6 frames per shot, start to end, tiled per shot. Look for hands through props or wheels, faces cut by the frame edge
  or hidden behind a post, poses that never land (a hand that never reaches the head), and captions or cards over faces.
- **Expressions blend, never switch:** a change of face is a mix from a to b over 0.25-0.4 s (lids, brows and gaze ease, the two mouths
  cross-fade). No forced blink that snaps shut. No pitch or sob jump on the same frame as an expression change.
- **Cards hold one place for their whole life.** A card never re-solves its position when the shot under it changes. Pick one spot that is
  clear in every shot it spans, inside the safe area including its paper padding.
- Mid-shot contact sheets and blind stills judges miss all of this. The motion check is its own gate.

## The long form after a viral Short is the next chapter, never a rerun (Ishaan, 2026-10-31, Gandhi Short at 80k views in 13 h; long form 3/10)
Ishaan: "I'm thinking not to repeat same wordings and stuff I already showed in shorts in main video, so that the people who see shorts also see the main
video and it doesn't feel repetitive." And: "animation is shitty 3/10, so many errors, bad colour grading, shorts was 10 times better. Make it even better than shorts."
- **No repeated lines or beats.** The long form shares the premise and the world with its Short. Every spoken line is new, and so is every featured beat.
  The Short's jokes, facts, climax and CTA are spent. The long form answers what the Short only promised: new facts, new tries, a new climax, a new ending.
  A Short viewer should meet something new within the first 5 s of the long form.
- **The long form must look better than its Short, never worse.** Porting a 9:16 film to 16:9 is a new look job, not a camera change.
  - Keep the Short's grade (exposure, contrast, palette per time of day) unless a change is proven better side by side.
  - Favour the close and medium shots that made the Short work. Never widen into shots that expose doll bodies, walk cycles or IK errors.
  - Night is lit by its practical light, with skin unchanged. It is never grey walls and mud.
- **Judge the long form against its own Short** in a blind side by side (same beats, matched frames) before anything reaches Ishaan.
  It ships only if it wins.
- **The other direction: Shorts cut from a long form that is already out (Ishaan, 2026-11-03, Stickman Games ep 1: "u can have cuts..
  long form had no view.. so make the best cuts so as people must watch long form.. first short is more like a teaser").** When the
  long form came first and has no audience yet, its Shorts ARE its best cuts: the job is to send viewers to it. The no-repeat rule
  above is for a long form made after a Short. Pick the moments that hook hardest, reframe them for 9:16 (faces in frame, captions
  inside the safe area, no burned-in 16:9 captions), and end every cut on an open question the long form answers (never its payoff).

  subject of every wide and two-shot, and is upscaled 1.8x. A film made in code is re-rendered natively at 1080x1920 for its Short: the
  same shot cameras (position and target), the vertical field opened about 1.8x so the subject sits whole with headroom, an off-axis lens

  cards, vignettes) rebuilt or graded in the Short's composer. Moovi: `kit/studio-kit.js ?vert=1&vk=&vpan=` and each page's `ST.vert` path.

  - **One continuous score designed for the teaser,** with the cuts placed on its beats. It is never each clip's own bed. The
    episode's effects come in only as chosen hits.
  - **Dialogue as J-cuts over the music:** a line starts before its picture, and a scene's sound never stops at a hard cut.
  - **A story a half-watching viewer follows:** who, what is at stake, the rule, the first loss, the escalation, the question.
    A line never arrives without its setup.

## Captions: themed, light, never a smudge (Ishaan, 2026-10-31, "u fucked up captions" on the Gandhi long form)

- **Never a blurred dark plate behind captions.** A soft brown pill blurred behind the words reads as a smudge on a bright pastel
  frame. Readability comes from the type itself: warm off-white with a hairline dark edge (about 5-6% of the size) and one
  tight soft shadow (blur about 12% of the size, low alpha). Less stroke and shadow, always; never a thick outline plus a big drop shadow.
- **Themed:** the caption borrows one object from the film's own world for its emphasis (the Gandhi film: the key word lands on a
  small torn khadi slip in Bapu's blue-black ink, the same paper and ink as the slips he writes on screen). One themed device,
  only on the key word, never on every word.
- **A long form's captions start from its Short's** (the one that went viral) and only improve on them; never a new, weaker system.
- Cards hold 2-3 words, no punctuation, and stay clear of faces.

## Merged voice takes, steady walking cameras, text that fits (Ishaan, 2026-11-01, "abrupt sentence cuts, 3d jitters, letter crops")

- **Cut a reused voice line at the take's own silence, never at "aligned end + a fixed pad".** Forced alignment marks a last word's
  end early (measured 0.1-1.2 s early on Viraj takes), so a fixed pad chops the last syllable or catches the next line's breath.
  Search forward from the aligned end for 120 ms below -44 dB, never past the next line of the same take; fade in 12 ms, out 45 ms
  (120 ms when the tail runs into the next take line). Gate: every join's last 30 ms below -40 dB.
- **A walking shot's camera follows the walker's path, never the head.** The head bobs with every step and a camera locked to it
  shakes the whole frame (the "3D jitter"); aim at root position + a fixed head height. No idle shake loop faster than about
  1 Hz (a 1.4 Hz pant read as trembling), and no move that snaps in under 0.4 s unless it is the joke.
- **Text written in the world always fits its paper.** A slip's font shrinks until its widest line fits 84% of the paper, the
  shot frames the whole slip with margin, no hand or prop rests on the words once they are written, and the caption hides while
  the slip is read (the slip is the line). Check every frame that shows written text, at full size.
- **Probe the overlay before rendering:** walk the film every 0.25 s and list every caption or card that leaves the frame or
  sits on a face; zero before the MP4.
